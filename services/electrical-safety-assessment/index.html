A useful assessment has a stated purpose and boundary

A condition concern, renovation decision or insurer request can each call for a different assessment. Agree which equipment and areas will be reviewed, whether testing is included and what written result will be provided. Concealed wiring and inaccessible equipment may limit conclusions. A general visit should not be represented as complete certification of everything in the property.

Prepare the scope for your East York property

Write down the single system or area you want reviewed, for example the panel, the kitchen circuits or outdoor receptacles by the driveway, and what prompted the request. Gather any earlier reports.

Ask each contractor what will be examined, whether any testing is included, what limits apply to concealed or inaccessible parts, and what the written result will say. Ask whether the quote excludes anything you assumed was covered.

What should the assessment establish?

This review covers one system or one area of an East York house. Its purpose, the equipment it includes and what it deliberately leaves out are stated. What is observed is written up, and what could not be reached is listed. Findings that need follow-up by a Licensed Electrical Contractor are marked, and the report does not present a limited review as a check of the whole property. Quotes compare fairly only on equal boundaries, since a narrower review may look lower in cost yet answer less. The report also says if it can go to an insurer or buyer.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Is a review of just one system worth comparing between contractors?

Yes, if each quote describes the same boundary. A limited review is a defined piece of work, so ask what is included, what is excluded and how any further findings would be handled.

Does a one-area review show whether the rest of the house is fine?

No. A review of one system reports on that system within its stated limits. Hidden wiring and equipment outside the agreed area are not covered, so ask what the written result will say about them.
